# Results and Findings
1. Depression among Students
The sample dataset had 4,464 students. Out of them, 1,201 (26.9%) students had depression, whereas 3,263 (73.1%) students did not have depression. From the above result, it is clear that most students of the dataset did not have depression.
2. Depression According to Gender
It was observed that female students had more cases of depression compared to male students. There were about 660 cases of depression among the female students and 550 cases among male students. On the other hand, there were a large number of students without depression among both genders.

Hence, it is clear that there was depression among both male and female students, but there were more cases of depression among female students.
3. Depression According to Relationship Status
According to the relationship status plot, most of the students had single relationship status. At the same time, the single relationship group was also found to have the largest number of cases of depression, which was about 1,000 students.

In case of the relationship, the number of depression cases was lower, while for complicated relationship, the lowest number of students was found.

4. Depression by Family Background
The results show that most of the students belonged to a middle-class family background, with lower-middle class and rich class family backgrounds following.
The middle class background category showed the largest number of students who showed depression. The lower-middle class background category also had a large number of students reporting depression. However, the rich class background category had a small number of students reporting depression.
It shows that students belonging to all three family-background categories suffered from depression.

5. Age Distribution
The age distribution results show that the age distribution of the students who had and those who did not have depression was almost the same.
The median age of both groups was about 23 years, with an age range of 17-28. It shows that there was no significant difference in the age distribution of both groups.

6. Current Semester Distribution
The box plot of the current semester distribution results for students with and without depression is almost identical, with a median semester of about 5 for both.

7. CGPA Distribution
From the CGPA plot, students with no depression had a higher median CGPA value (about 3.50) than those with depression (3.42).
Additionally, it is worth noting that there were wider CGPA distributions among students with depression, which indicates some differences in academic performance, although the distributions have some similarities.

8. Cost Without Study
The cost distributions are fairly similar between the two groups. It was observed that students with depression had a median value of about 10,000, while those with no depression had a median cost value of about 9,300.
There were also unusual high values for both distributions.

9. Summary of Results
The exploratory data analysis has shown that depression was prevalent among various genders, marital status, background classes, ages, and academic semesters.

Some of the important findings were:
- More depression cases were reported by female students than by male students.
- Single students were the most represented students reporting depression.
- Middle-class students had the most depression cases.
- Age and semester distributions were similar in both groups of students.
- Students with no depression had higher CGPA values than those with depression.
- There was similarity in the cost distribution between the two groups.
